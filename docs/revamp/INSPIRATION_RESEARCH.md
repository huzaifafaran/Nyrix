# Enterprise website inspiration and Nyrix approach

Research date: 16 October 2026. Status: discovery and recommendation; no visual direction is approved/locked and no website redesign was implemented.

## 1. Recommendation

Build a calm, credible enterprise AI company website that makes Nyrix's capabilities, actual work, people, and published thinking easy to investigate. The strongest reference combination is **Quantexa for decision-intelligence explanation, Thoughtworks for company/insights architecture, Faculty for company credibility, and Afiniti for the comprehensive footer principle**.

This is an original direction informed by references, not a collage of their visual identities. Nyrix has a mix of products and services; that relationship needs to be understandable before styling begins.

## 2. Evidence and limitations

- Reviewed public page content/navigation through web research and inspected local Nyrix code. Sources below are specific pages, not a claim to have reviewed every site on the internet.
- Pinterest is useful for discovery; follow the attribution to the original project. Behance/Dribbble presentations are design references, not evidence of working navigation, mobile quality, accessibility, or business outcomes.
- Live-source observations below concern retrieved content and structure. Full rendered visual rhythm, interaction quality, exact font usage, current breakpoints, and performance were not verified. No exact live-site font/color specification is asserted.
- Some Behance/Pinterest media was blocked or unavailable. Candidates with limited access are labeled instead of receiving an invented visual diagnosis. The source dates of concepts are stated where available.
- Nyrix live-browser access was declined by the access gate. Its assessment is based on current local source; no workaround was used to access that origin.
- Hallmark audit/study principles informed the critique and reference selection. This is comparative research, not a completed pixel-level Hallmark DNA extraction or a locked `design.md`.

## 3. Primary enterprise references

### A. Quantexa — strongest business-category match

[Live website](https://www.quantexa.com/) · [Designer case study on Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/shots/27047801--Case-Study-Enterprise-Website-Design-for-Quantexa)

Observed: decision intelligence is introduced through connected data, business decisions, platform capabilities, use cases, customer stories, leadership, and resources. Product-interface image descriptions accompany explanations of the system.

Nyrix takeaway: make “Decision Sciences” concrete through understandable use cases and evidence. Give products, services, company, and resources distinct destinations. My assessment is that this is the most relevant category reference.

Do not carry over its full menu complexity, finance/government positioning, claimed outcomes, or assets. The designer's reported business results do not establish that a redesign caused them.

### B. Thoughtworks — strongest expertise and publishing architecture

[Company website](https://www.thoughtworks.com/) · [Podcasts](https://www.thoughtworks.com/insights/podcasts)

Observed: separate pathways for capabilities, working approach, client work, and insights. Articles, publications, books, and podcasts are explicit content categories.

Nyrix takeaway: authored thinking and podcasts should belong to the company's normal navigation and publishing system. Use a compact version of this architecture suited to the amount of real Nyrix content.

Do not copy the breadth of a global consultancy or build empty sections to look larger.

### C. Faculty — strongest company/product/service balance

[Homepage](https://faculty.ai/en-gb) · [Company page](https://faculty.ai/en-gb/company)

Observed: the site distinguishes AI services, a named product, industries, impact, insights, and company information. The homepage highlights applied work and current thinking, while Company is a dedicated destination.

Nyrix takeaway: explain the organization and expertise alongside what it sells. Give team/advisors real space with approved photography and biographies. This people-first application is my recommendation, not a claim that Faculty's exact structure must be replicated.

### D. Cohere — useful separation of technical offerings and enterprise buying

[Homepage](https://cohere.com/) · [Customer stories](https://cohere.com/customer-stories)

Observed: products, solutions, research, resources, and company are separated. Deployment/security material and customer stories give buyers paths beyond a demo CTA.

Nyrix takeaway: products and consulting should have different explanations and appropriate next steps. Show actual product screens where they clarify the offering.

Do not imply Nyrix has Cohere's model/platform infrastructure, certifications, deployment options, or partner relationships.

### E. Afiniti — required stakeholder baseline

[Homepage and footer](https://www.afiniti.com/)

Observed: product, company, guides, and resource groups appear in the footer, with contact/legal destinations. The site gives offerings and supporting content separate routes.

Nyrix takeaway: keep the mandatory all-in-one footer as a real site directory. Its link groups should reflect Nyrix's actual content. The current website does not prove what the stakeholder's preferred older version looked like.

### F. IBM Consulting — useful service categorization

[Consulting website](https://www.ibm.com/consulting)

Observed: services are organized around business/technology concerns, alongside delivery explanation, partnerships, and thought leadership.

Nyrix takeaway: describe the problem, intended audience, engagement, and deliverables for each service. Keep this proportional to Nyrix's real scope; institutional credibility should come from evidence rather than borrowed scale language.

### G. Anthropic — supplementary company/publication reference

[Company homepage](https://www.anthropic.com/)

Use as a secondary source when evaluating how an AI company surfaces company information and published work. It is not the recommended complete Nyrix template. Its familiar brand treatments should not become a shortcut to an original enterprise identity.

## 5. What the references change about our approach

The following is my synthesis from the sources, not a claim that every referenced site follows the same rules.

| Current Nyrix signal | Recommended enterprise treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Large centered slogans and repeated demo prompts | Explain the company, audience, relevant problem, evidence, and next step. |
| Homepage holds company, detailed products, services, agents, case studies, and contact | Homepage previews a deliberately organized set of full destinations. |
| Products, bespoke delivery, and agents compete at the same level | Distinguish reusable products, consulting/implementation capabilities, and example use cases. |
| Company is mostly mission/values/stats | Company includes real leadership/team, advisors, experience, and a clear working approach. |
| Proof includes generic badges and unsupported-in-this-task statistics | Use approved case-study detail, real images, attributable evidence, and precise qualifications. |
| Purple/icon/motion patterns dominate brand expression | Let content, photography, product evidence, and consistent typography build identity; use accent color selectively. |
| Footer has columns but incomplete destinations | Shared full directory with actual offerings, company, resources, contact, and published legal pages. |
| Services are often the destination for product-sounding calls to action | Give each CTA a label and destination that match the visitor's intent. |

## 6. Proposed visual direction

**Recommended: restrained technology company with an editorial content layer.** Here “editorial” means readable articles, genuine authorship, good image/caption handling, and clear content hierarchy. It does not require a newspaper layout, oversized serif typography, decorative labels, or a cream-and-terracotta palette.

- Keep Nyrix's recognizable identity and consider retaining purple as a controlled accent. Existing purple is not inherently the problem.
- Use neutral light surfaces, dark readable text, clear section separation, and a substantial footer. Specific tokens remain a design decision, not an extracted fact about the references.
- Start by testing the existing Plus Jakarta Sans with calmer weights and sentence-case headings. Change the family only if the chosen direction benefits from it.
- Prefer an informative, content-sized opening. A left-biased headline with a relevant real product/work image is a candidate; a strong text-only treatment is preferable when suitable approved imagery is missing.
- Real team/advisor photography should be consistently framed and visibly distinct from agent illustrations. Include names, roles, expertise, and useful biographies.
- Use proper product screenshots and restrained system diagrams where they explain something. Do not add fake dashboards, fabricated numbers, floating 3D orbs, or generic robot art for decoration.
- Reserve animation for state transitions and useful feedback. No parallax; preserve a coherent site with immediately readable content.
- Vary layouts by purpose: case-study reading, team directory, product explanation, article, and podcast episode need different content structures within one shared design system.

An alternative direction is a more product-led enterprise technology presentation influenced by Quantexa/Cohere. Use that only if the approved positioning makes products the main business story. The recommended hybrid currently fits the repository's actual mix of products, advisory, and implementation work better.

## 7. Proposed page structure

This is a working recommendation, not an approved route migration.

```text
Home /
├── Company /company
│   ├── Team /company/team
│   └── Advisors /company/advisors
├── Solutions /solutions
│   └── Existing four service routes
├── Products /products
│   └── Approved product detail pages /products/{slug}
├── Our Work /case-studies
│   ├── /case-studies/cleanroom-ems
│   └── /case-studies/teachmeback
├── Insights /resources
│   ├── Articles & Publications /resources/articles
│   └── Podcasts /resources/podcasts
├── Contact /contact
├── Agent library /agents
└── Privacy /privacy-policy
```

Header candidate: Solutions, Products, Our Work, Company, Insights, and a clear contact action. Keep the agent library discoverable through relevant product/solution content and the footer rather than letting it define the entire company.

Footer groups: Offerings, Company, Resources, Contact, and Legal, with approved product/service links and actual pages. Do not add nonexistent Careers/Trust/Policies simply to fill columns.

| Existing destination | Recommended handling |
| --- | --- |
| `/` | Retain; shorten and reorganize its content. |
| `/agents` | Retain; clarify library role and cross-link from relevant offerings. |
| Four existing service paths | Retain initially under Solutions navigation; redirect only if deliberately changed. |
| Two existing case-study paths | Retain and connect to the new case-study index. |
| `/privacy-policy` | Retain and reconcile styling/approved text. |
| `#about`, `#products`, `#services`, `#case-studies`, `#contact` | Plan a transition to dedicated pages. URL fragments are handled by the browser, so server redirects alone cannot migrate old hash links; use real anchor targets or a small intentional client mapping. |

Products should link to related case studies where evidence exists. Case studies should link back to relevant services. Articles/episodes should expose authors/guests and connect to appropriate expertise. Team/advisor pages should be reachable from Company and the footer. Avoid orphan pages or fabricated associations.

## 8. Recommended execution order

1. **Inventory and evidence:** use the code index; confirm product/service positioning, existing claims, approved assets, and strongest work.
2. **Information architecture:** finalize the sitemap, buyer journeys, primary CTA, and URL preservation plan with site-architecture guidance.
3. **Content models:** define people, advisors, products, case studies, articles, and episodes using the real available content. Record gaps explicitly.
4. **Design study:** use Hallmark for a focused rendered study of the strongest references once screenshots/browser access are available. Keep an observations/takeaways/rejections record rather than adopting a whole site wholesale.
5. **Representative design set:** design homepage, Company/Team, one solution, one case study, one article/episode template, and the shared footer. Testing only the hero will not establish an enterprise system.
6. **Shared system:** settle typography, colors, grids, buttons, navigation, content widths, and image treatment from those representative pages. Do not rotate themes across routes.
7. **Incremental implementation:** keep React/Router/Tailwind, preserve meaningful content/URLs, build shared templates, then populate remaining pages.
8. **Verification:** Hallmark critique, accessibility/interface review, meaningful browser flows, mobile/desktop layouts, build, actual metadata/indexability, and link/redirect checks.

The first implementation milestone should be a navigable homepage + Company/Team + one solution + one case study with the comprehensive header/footer. Article/podcast structures belong in the initial architecture and must be completed with real content before the revamp is called finished.

## 9. Decisions already established vs. still open

Established: enterprise/minimal, multiple pages, team/advisors, podcasts/publications, no parallax, mandatory comprehensive footer, extensive Hallmark use, and preserving relevant existing work.

Open: exact business positioning, product priority, available people/content assets, primary conversion wording, final typography/tokens, and launch schedule. These should guide the first representative designs; they do not justify restarting discovery or asking the user to repeat the established brief.
